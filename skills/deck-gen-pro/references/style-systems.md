# Style Systems

Use these systems during the visual style interview. They are reusable starting points, not rigid templates. Recommend 2-3 options based on the deck topic, audience, evidence density, source material, and asset quality.

Sources to consult when refreshing this file: [Pitch's public template gallery](https://pitch.com/templates), [Pitch's presentation design guidance](https://pitch.com/guides/presentation/design-your-presentation), and [Pitch's template creation process](https://pitch.com/blog/designing-presentation-templates). Pitch frames templates as reusable slide layouts and styles as consistent rules for color, fonts, and spacing; its template process also emphasizes discovery, plain-text outline, visual exploration, feedback, and iteration.

## Selection Rules

- Pick a style that matches the audience's decision context, not only the topic's surface aesthetics.
- Prefer restrained systems for executive, financial, legal, technical, or board audiences.
- Prefer image-led systems for consumer, place, travel, fashion, food, art, real estate, people, sports, and product-showcase decks.
- Use high-contrast accent colors only for the few things the audience must notice.
- Keep the chosen palette to 2-4 working colors plus neutrals.
- Avoid repeating one layout across every slide; vary rhythm while keeping the same grammar.

## 1. Editorial Strategy

Best for: strategy narratives, market maps, thought leadership, keynote-style briefings.

Layout:
- Large claim title on the upper left or full-width top band.
- One dominant proof object per slide: diagram, quote, hero image, or chart.
- Occasional magazine-style chapter openers with full-bleed image and compact text.
- Use generous margins and strong asymmetry.

Palette:
- Warm white `#F7F3EC`
- Ink `#161616`
- Deep olive `#3B4A3F`
- Signal red `#C43E2F`

Typography:
- Serif or humanist headline, clean sans body.
- Short titles with strong line breaks.

Accents:
- Thin editorial rules, pull quotes, page markers, restrained red annotations.

Image direction:
- Documentary, real-world, textured, not stocky.
- Prefer one meaningful image over decorative mosaics.

## 2. Founder Pitch Minimal

Best for: startup fundraising, product vision, company narrative, early-stage business decks.

Layout:
- Big claim at top, proof below.
- Alternate title-only conviction slides with traction/data slides.
- Use sparse iconography and simple architecture diagrams.
- Keep one idea per slide, with strong whitespace.

Palette:
- Near black `#101114`
- White `#FFFFFF`
- Electric blue `#2F6BFF`
- Soft gray `#E8EAEE`

Typography:
- Confident sans headline, regular sans body.
- Large numbers and concise labels.

Accents:
- Single accent color for traction, progress, and selected metrics.
- Small chips for customer segments or product modules.

Image direction:
- Product screenshots, customer logos, founder/product photography only when relevant.

## 3. Premium Brand Monochrome

Best for: luxury, fashion, portfolio, architecture, high-end consumer brand, personal brand.

Layout:
- Full-bleed or half-bleed imagery with minimal text.
- Slow rhythm: fewer objects, more negative space.
- Use centered title slides sparingly; body slides stay grid-aligned.
- Strong image cropping and consistent captions.

Palette:
- Bone `#F4EFE6`
- Charcoal `#202020`
- Stone `#B9B2A7`
- Metallic accent `#A9854F`

Typography:
- Elegant serif headlines, neutral sans captions.
- Avoid heavy bold except for numbers.

Accents:
- Fine borders, small labels, subtle foil-like accent.

Image direction:
- High-resolution, tactile, carefully cropped.
- Avoid collages unless the subject is a collection.

## 4. Data Room Executive

Best for: financial updates, board decks, operating reviews, market sizing, analytics summaries.

Layout:
- Dense but calm grids.
- Strong title claim, then chart/table/bridge with footnotes.
- Use repeated chart grammar and consistent axis/legend placement.
- Include appendix-style backup slides for detail.

Palette:
- White `#FFFFFF`
- Slate `#27313D`
- Cool gray `#D9DEE6`
- Teal `#168A8F`
- Amber alert `#D99A20`

Typography:
- One sans family with strict size hierarchy.
- Titles should read like findings, not section labels.

Accents:
- Highlight bars, variance tags, confidence bands, callout boxes.

Image direction:
- Mostly charts, tables, and diagrams.
- Use photos only for market context or customer evidence.

## 5. Product System Blueprint

Best for: SaaS, platform, AI tools, developer products, product strategy, roadmap decks.

Layout:
- System diagrams, workflow lanes, before/after screens.
- Use modular blocks and connector lines.
- Alternate between architecture maps, user journey slides, and proof metrics.
- Show screenshots in device/browser frames only when legibility survives.

Palette:
- Graphite `#171A1F`
- Cloud `#F6F8FB`
- Cyan `#22B8CF`
- Violet `#7C5CFF`
- Lime `#B7E35F`

Typography:
- Technical sans or grotesk headline, compact body.
- Use monospace only for code-like labels.

Accents:
- Nodes, connectors, status dots, layer labels, route arrows.

Image direction:
- Real product UI, architecture diagrams, workflow captures.
- Avoid generic isometric tech art.

## 6. Research Lab

Best for: academic, science, policy, UX research, insight reports, healthcare, education.

Layout:
- Hypothesis or finding title.
- Evidence panels: method, sample, quote, chart, implication.
- Use calm section dividers and clear source notes.
- Leave room for caveats and methodology.

Palette:
- Off-white `#FAFAF7`
- Deep green `#1F4D3A`
- Pale mint `#DCEFE6`
- Blue gray `#637A89`
- Coral accent `#E46F5A`

Typography:
- Scholarly serif or readable sans for headlines.
- Body text slightly larger than typical dense decks for readability.

Accents:
- Highlighted evidence tags, quote marks, annotated findings.

Image direction:
- Field imagery, lab/context photos, diagrams, respondent quotes.
- Avoid over-polished corporate photography.

## 7. Creative Campaign Pop

Best for: marketing campaigns, social launches, creator decks, entertainment, youth brands.

Layout:
- Bold title compositions, stickers/shapes, kinetic diagonals.
- Use alternating dense and simple slides to avoid fatigue.
- Moodboard grids and concept territories can use multiple images.
- Keep final recommendation slides simpler and more decisive.

Palette:
- Cream `#FFF6E8`
- Punch pink `#FF4F8B`
- Cobalt `#2458FF`
- Acid green `#C7FF2E`
- Black `#111111`

Typography:
- Bold display headline with simple body.
- Use playful type treatments only for short text.

Accents:
- Big numerals, labels, swatches, torn-paper or sticker motifs if topic fits.

Image direction:
- Campaign visuals, generated concepts, social screenshots, moodboard grids.
- Keep provenance and generation prompts in the asset manifest.

## 8. Consultancy Clarity

Best for: proposals, transformation programs, recommendations, process decks, professional services.

Layout:
- Structured title, key takeaway, and decision options.
- 2x2 matrices, roadmaps, swimlanes, issue trees, comparison tables.
- Use repeated navigation markers and section dividers.
- Favor clarity over decoration.

Palette:
- White `#FFFFFF`
- Navy `#17233C`
- Ice `#EDF4FA`
- Sky `#4BA3F2`
- Orange accent `#F28C28`

Typography:
- Clean corporate sans.
- Use clear section labels and strong action titles.

Accents:
- Decision markers, numbered steps, thin dividers, sparing icons.

Image direction:
- Mostly diagrams and charts.
- Use client/product imagery only when it strengthens specificity.

## 9. Immersive Place Story

Best for: travel, real estate, destination, hospitality, city, venue, museum, restaurant decks.

Layout:
- Full-bleed scene openers.
- Map/detail/image triptychs.
- Use visual rhythm: wide establishing shot, detail grid, practical info.
- Text overlays need strong contrast and quiet placement.

Palette:
- Deep forest `#12372A`
- Warm sand `#E9D7B8`
- Clay `#B8643C`
- Mist `#EDF2EF`
- Night `#111516`

Typography:
- Serif or elegant sans titles with compact labels.
- Captions should be minimal and useful.

Accents:
- Map pins, route lines, itinerary markers, local texture swatches.

Image direction:
- Real images are strongly preferred.
- Generated imagery is acceptable only for speculative concepts, clearly noted.

## 10. Workshop Canvas

Best for: facilitation, training, innovation workshops, design sprints, internal alignment.

Layout:
- Big question title, then canvases, sticky-note grids, priority maps.
- Use spacious interaction zones and clear labels.
- Include agenda, activity, output, and decision slides.
- Preserve readability when printed or shared as PDF.

Palette:
- White `#FFFFFF`
- Ink `#202124`
- Pale yellow `#FFF2A8`
- Soft blue `#CDE7FF`
- Magenta accent `#D94B8A`

Typography:
- Friendly sans headline and body.
- Large labels for exercises.

Accents:
- Sticky notes, voting dots, facilitator callouts, numbered activity badges.

Image direction:
- Diagrams, canvases, screenshots of workshop inputs, simple icons.
- Photos only for context or human energy.
